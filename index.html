Hi, I'm Ehsan (Ethan)
Mahmoodi

Manufacturing & Industrial Engineer · Optimization and Simulation Developer

I work at the point where a production system meets the software that runs it. On one side I am an industrial engineer: I model factories, hospitals, plants and fleets, find where they actually lose time and money, and redesign them. On the other side I am a developer: I build the simulation models, optimisation algorithms and AI tools that make those decisions reproducible instead of one-off consulting slides.

Sixteen years of this, across Iran, Sweden and New Zealand — a sulfuric acid plant, two hospitals, an offshore supply fleet, a 10 MW solar farm, assembly lines at Volvo Penta and Husqvarna — and sixteen peer-reviewed publications along the way.

Two Roles, One Problem
Most people who can model a factory cannot ship the software, and most people who can ship the software have never stood on a shop floor. The projects below are deliberately split to show both.
Role One

Manufacturing & Industrial Engineering

Diagnosing real systems: where the bottleneck is, what a stockout actually costs, whether a maintenance policy pays for itself, how many vessels a field really needs.

  • Discrete-event simulation of production, healthcare and logistics systems
  • Throughput and bottleneck analysis, including shifting bottlenecks
  • Maintenance strategy design — breakdown, preventive, predictive
  • Assembly line balancing and mixed-model sequencing
  • Capacity, layout and staffing studies before capital is committed
  • Techno-economic feasibility: NPV, IRR, payback on plant investments
  • Multi-criteria decision analysis (TOPSIS, AHP, ANP, DEA)
Role Two

Optimization & Software Development

Building the tools rather than buying them: solvers, metaheuristics, simulation engines, dashboards and AI assistants that put a decision in someone's hands.

  • Multi-objective optimisation — NSGA-II, MOVNS, constraint programming
  • Metaheuristics and matheuristics for scheduling and routing
  • Machine learning surrogates that replace expensive simulation runs
  • Python, Julia, C/C++ — from research prototype to packaged application
  • Simulation engines in Python (SimPy, Salabim, custom DES) and Arena
  • LLM and agent systems: Azure OpenAI, Semantic Kernel, multi-agent frameworks
  • Streamlit, FastAPI, Docker, Azure deployment

Maintenance Optimisation, Sulfuric Acid Plant

🏭
Bafgh acid unit · MSc thesis, later rebuilt in Python

Three maintenance strategies compared over eighteen scenarios on a 24/7 plant, ranked by TOPSIS across downtime, cost, crew utilisation and spares. Predictive maintenance cut annual downtime by 96.6% and monthly maintenance cost by 91.5%. Later rebuilt as a Python NSGA-II model for crew sizing.

Featured design case study

SME-Scale Biodiesel Production for Northern Iran

My 2009 bachelor project was a study of transesterification chemistry — acid and base catalysis, supercritical methanol, enzymatic routes. Useful, but it stopped at the chemistry. This is the project it should have been: a 3,000 t/yr plant sized for a cooperative in Gilan or Mazandaran, running on used cooking oil and regional rapeseed.

Feedstock availability, process train, mass and energy balance, capital and operating cost, glycerol as a second revenue line, and the payback period that decides whether any of it happens. Engineering only becomes a project when the numbers close.

Digital ID Card Distribution Centres

🧾
2013 · national rollout

Process design for the centres where citizens actually collect a digital ID: the service steps, the data transitions behind them, and what the data centre has to carry. Applicants do not arrive as one population — first-time, renewal, replacement and assisted cases have different service times, so the queue behaves differently depending on the local mix.

The model produced processing time, time in system and staffing per counter, and from those the question the rollout actually needed answered: how many centres each region requires given its population.

Digital Twin for Network Maintenance Planning

📡
2020 · national wired and wireless estate

A live digital twin of a nationwide telecom network — 3,880 monitored elements across xDSL, wireless backhaul, IP backbone, server farms and enterprise links — rolled up from element state to provincial availability, with round-trip time and packet loss tracked alongside it.

Two failure modes had to be planned for differently: a wired run fails outright, a wireless hop degrades gradually. The twin drives preventive and corrective policy per link type, with crew routing and spares held against how critical each link is.
