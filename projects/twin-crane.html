Stakeholder engagement · business problem

Two cranes, one rail, and a machining line waiting on both

Volvo Cars · a fully automated engine machining line.

On a fully automated machining line, engine bodies move between machines on two overhead cranes that share one runway rail. The cranes work back and forth all shift, and they cannot pass each other. Every minute a machine waits for a crane is machining capacity lost — so the question was not whether the cranes could be scheduled, but how to make each crane decision so the line keeps moving.

Diagnostic analysis

Two ways a shared rail fails

The non-crossing constraint turns a simple dispatch rule into a trap.

At every moment the left crane must stay left of the right one: positionC1(t) < positionC2(t). Standard greedy dispatching fails in one of two ways:

  • Physical deadlock. Both cranes accept overlapping jobs, meet in the middle loaded and facing each other, and freeze — and a frozen pair halts the whole bay.
  • Starvation. A dispatcher made cautious to avoid deadlock reserves huge safety zones for one crane while the other sits idle, and the job queue backs up anyway.
Predictive modelling · simulation

A discrete-event testbed you can watch

Salabim for the simulation engine, Pygame for a 60 FPS industrial dashboard.

The simulation logic and the visualisation are fully separated: Salabim handles the event queue, yielding and timing; Pygame renders the rail, the cranes and their reserved zones. The animation is also how the model is checked — a deadlock or a starved crane is visible the moment it happens, long before it shows up in a throughput table.

Prescriptive · decision rules and optimisation

Deadlock-free by construction, then optimised

A rule-based dispatcher as the safe baseline, and a MILP for the best sequence.
  1. Just-in-time assignment. A crane is given its next job only when it becomes idle, so it reserves as little rail as possible and leaves the rest of the line open.
  2. Spatial look-ahead. Before a job is dispatched, the whole zone it will need is projected; if it intersects the other crane’s active zone, the job waits.
  3. Escape routing. If an idle crane blocks an active one, the active crane computes a safe escape track and sends a relocate command that moves the idle crane out of the way.
  4. Mixed-integer optimisation. Rules are safe but not optimal under heavy load, so the system was also formulated as a MILP that minimises total empty travel — and with it energy and cycle time.

The simulation testbed and the rule-based dispatcher are public. The MILP back end (Gurobi / PuLP) is not, for confidentiality reasons.
