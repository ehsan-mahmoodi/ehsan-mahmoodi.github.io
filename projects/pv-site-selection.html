The Price of Not Putting All Your Megawatts Behind One Substation

Business problem

500 candidate sites. Which 220 MW do you build?

Engineering basis from the Nir PV plant, Yazd · Kish Solar Trading Co.

A developer’s pre-study screens a region down to about 500 candidate points, each with irradiation, terrain, land price and distances to the grid and the road network. They now need a 220 MW portfolio within a budget of €190M. Ranking sites by one number does not work, because the criteria disagree by construction: the best irradiation is in the south, far from substations; the cheapest land is far from roads; substations sit near towns, where land costs most.

What is real and what is synthetic. The engineering and cost basis is real: the 87% performance ratio and the irradiation range come from the 10 MW Nir PV plant, whose project profile and financial model I authored at Kish Solar Trading Co.; degradation, EPC cost per kW, grid connection cost and land footprint are industry-standard figures, stated as named constants. The 500 candidate sites are synthetic, generated in 2026 with the correlations real geography has — without them, the three objectives would stop conflicting.
Predictive model

Three objectives, including the one usually left out

25-year energy, capital cost, and concentration risk.

Total capital costminimiseEPC, land, grid connection, access road, earthworks
Concentration riskminimiseHerfindahl index over substations, plus geographic clustering

A portfolio that puts 220 MW behind one substation is cheaper and sunnier — and one transformer failure removes all of it. Without the risk objective the optimiser recommends exactly that, and a lender rejects it. Constraints: capacity within ±6% of target, the capital budget, spare capacity at each substation, no more than 30% behind any one substation, and at least 12 km between selected sites.

Prescriptive optimisation

NSGA-II, written for this problem

A binary selection vector over 500 sites, in NumPy, with no solver dependency.
  • Constrained domination. A feasible portfolio always beats an infeasible one; between two infeasible ones, the smaller violation wins — so the population migrates into the feasible region instead of being culled.
  • Repair operator. A random selection of 500 sites is essentially never within ±6% of a capacity target, so children are repaired by adding or dropping sites until they land in the band.
  • Crowding distance keeps the front spread rather than bunched at one end.

From 0 feasible portfolios at generation 0 to the whole population feasible by generation 40, with the risk objective still improving until about generation 140.
